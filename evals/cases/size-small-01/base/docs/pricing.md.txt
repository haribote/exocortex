# Pricing pipeline

A quote is built in one pass through `src/pricing/quote.ts`.
The stages are fixed and each one hands minor units to the next.

1. **Validate** the request. Quantity must be a positive integer within
   `MAX_LINE_QUANTITY`.
2. **Resolve** the catalog entry with `entryFor`. Retired SKUs raise
   `InactiveSkuError`.
3. **Scale** the catalog's major-unit `unitAmount` into minor units. This is the
   one and only conversion in the pipeline.
4. **Discount** with `applyTier` from `src/pricing/tier.ts`. It takes minor units
   and returns minor units.
5. **Tax** the discounted amount with the region rate from `src/pricing/tax.ts`.
6. **Assemble** the `Quote`.

## Why the unit rule matters

The catalog stores major units because that is what finance edits.
Everything downstream — the invoice service, the revenue report, the ledger
export — assumes integers in minor units.
A second call to `toMinorUnits` on an already-scaled value multiplies the amount
by 100 in `USD` and `EUR` and by 1 in `JPY`.

The failure is quiet. The quote still returns, the invoice still posts, and the
mistake only shows up when a customer reads the tax line.

## Tier discounts

Tier rules live in `src/pricing/tier.ts` and are keyed by `TierId`.
A tier applies only when the line quantity reaches its `minQuantity`, so a
`scale` customer buying two seats pays the list price.
`TIER_DISCOUNTS_ENABLED=0` turns the stage into a pass-through, which is how the
pricing team ships a rule change dark.
