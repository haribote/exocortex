# ADR 0009: one conversion to minor units, at the catalog edge

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-03-18
- Supersedes: ADR 0004 (currency handling in the quote builder)

## Context

Finance edits the catalog in major units. A standard seat is `18`, not `1800`,
because that is what a spreadsheet export looks like and what an auditor expects
to read. Everything downstream of the catalog wants integers: an invoice line
that is `1799.9999999999998` cents cannot be posted, and a ledger export that
carries floats cannot be reconciled.

Before this decision the conversion happened wherever it was convenient. The
quote builder scaled the catalog amount, the invoice service scaled the line it
received in case the caller had not, and the ledger export scaled again if the
value looked "too small". Each of those guards was written by someone who had
been burned by a caller that did not scale, and together they made the unit of
any given number unknowable without reading three files.

Two production incidents came out of that arrangement: INC-341, where an EU
customer was overcharged by a factor of 100, and INC-352, where a JPY customer
was undercharged by the same factor. Both returned a well-formed response and
were reported by the customer rather than by an alert.

## Decision

**A major-unit amount is converted to minor units exactly once, at the edge
where it enters the pricing pipeline, and never again.**

Concretely:

1. `toMinorUnits` in `src/pricing/money.ts` is the only conversion function, and
   the only legitimate caller inside the pipeline is `src/pricing/quote.ts`,
   where it is applied to the catalog's `unitAmount`.
2. Every stage after that — `src/pricing/tier.ts`, `src/pricing/tax.ts`, the
   invoice service, the revenue report, the ledger export — takes minor units
   and returns minor units. A stage that changes the unit of its input is a bug
   in that stage.
3. Defensive re-scaling is forbidden. A function that suspects its caller may
   have handed it major units calls `assertMinorUnits`, which throws, rather
   than silently repairing the value.
4. Names carry the unit. A number in minor units is named `*Minor` at every
   boundary where the reader cannot see where it came from.

## Consequences

The rule is cheap to follow and expensive to check. Nothing in the type system
distinguishes minor units from major units — both are `number` — so the only
enforcement is review and the naming convention.

That makes the pipeline order in `src/pricing/quote.ts` load-bearing, and it
puts the burden on review rather than on the compiler.

The JPY asymmetry also makes unit mistakes hard to see in tests, since a
currency with an exponent of 0 hides them. `test/pricing/quote.test.ts`
therefore prices in USD, and the tier rules in `tier.test.ts` assert on exact
minor-unit integers rather than on ratios.

## Alternatives considered

**A branded `Minor` type.** Rejected for now. It would touch every signature in
`src/pricing/` and does not survive `JSON.parse`. Revisit after a third
incident.
